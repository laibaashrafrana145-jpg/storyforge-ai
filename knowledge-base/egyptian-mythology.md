# Egyptian Mythology — Knowledge Base

## Gods and Deities
- Ra: The sun god, king of the gods, travels through the underworld each night on his solar barque
- Osiris: God of the afterlife, death, and resurrection. Killed by Set, resurrected by Isis
- Isis: Goddess of magic, healing, and protection. Wife of Osiris, mother of Horus
- Horus: God of the sky and kingship, falcon-headed. Every pharaoh was considered an incarnation of Horus
- Set: God of chaos, storms, and the desert. Killed his brother Osiris out of jealousy
- Anubis: Jackal-headed god of mummification and the afterlife
- Thoth: Ibis-headed god of wisdom, writing, and the moon
- Bastet: Cat goddess of home, fertility, and protection
- Sekhmet: Lion-headed goddess of war and healing
- Amun: King of the gods in the New Kingdom, patron god of Thebes

## Sacred Places
- Thebes: Capital of the New Kingdom, home of the Karnak and Luxor temples
- Memphis: Ancient capital, home of the Ptah temple
- Heliopolis: Center of sun worship, home of the obelisks
- Valley of the Kings: Royal burial ground on the west bank of the Nile
- Abu Simbel: Temple carved into rock by Ramesses II

## Magic and Powers
- Heka: The fundamental magical force of the universe
- Amulets: Objects imbued with protective magic, worn by both living and dead
- Spells from the Book of the Dead: Guide souls through the underworld
- Shabtis: Small figurines that serve the deceased in the afterlife
- The Eye of Ra: Symbol of protection, royal power, and good health

## Culture and Society
- Pharaoh considered a living god, intermediary between humans and gods
- Mummification preserved the body for the afterlife
- Canopic jars stored organs removed during mummification
- The Nile flooding each year was considered a gift from the gods
- Priests served as intermediaries between humans and the gods

## Architecture
- Pyramids: Royal tombs, built as stairways to the heavens
- Obelisks: Tall pointed stone monuments representing the rays of the sun
- Sphinxes: Guardian creatures with human head and lion body
- Hypostyle halls: Massive columned halls in temples
- Pylons: Massive gateway towers at temple entrances
